What the app is

  • A structured memory for health and daily-life context.
  • A reflection tool that helps users compare categories over time.
  • An appointment-preparation tool with user-approved summaries.
  • An offline-resilient mobile app with optional secure sync.

What the app is not

  • Not a diagnosis or a replacement for a clinician.
  • Not a medication-prescribing system.
  • Not proof that one event caused another.
  • Not currently secure for real personal health information.

Six first-class tracking domains

One timeline, multiple kinds of context

Core user loop

  1. Record something quickly.
  2. Review category and cross-category trends.
  3. Ask a focused reflection question.
  4. Correct or annotate the interpretation.
  5. Select what belongs in an appointment brief.
  6. Share or export only with explicit user action.

Required trust behaviors

  • Every insight shows its date range and source entries.
  • Raw entries remain separate from generated summaries.
  • Users can edit, exclude, or delete generated material.
  • Missing data and uncertainty are stated plainly.

Business plan hypotheses

Sell appointment preparedness and trustworthy synthesis—not chat volume.

Packaging hypothesis

  • Free: basic tracking and local history.
  • Consumer subscription: AI-assisted summaries, appointment briefs, advanced exports, and longer history.
  • Family/caregiver plan: explicit permissions and shared workflows.
  • Later pilots: clinics, coaches, or patient-support organizations after privacy and workflow validation.
  • Research partnerships: governed access to consented, de-identified datasets or analyses—not ownership of identifiable user records.

Durable advantage hypothesis

  • User-controlled longitudinal data across six domains.
  • Evidence-linked corrections and summaries over time.
  • A trusted appointment workflow rather than a generic chatbot.
  • Reusable personal context that remains portable across AI providers.

Long-term institutional opportunity

A participant-consented real-world evidence platform

At sufficient scale, MultiUnity could produce a uniquely rich longitudinal dataset connecting symptoms, mood, food, interventions, vitals, and everyday observations. Universities, nonprofit research groups, public-health organizations, and life-science companies could become paying customers for approved studies, aggregate analyses, governed data access, or consented participant recruitment. This revenue could help offset product development, privacy and security infrastructure, marketing, and responsible customer-acquisition costs.

Commercial model

  • License purpose-limited aggregate datasets or analysis—not unrestricted raw-data resale.
  • Charge for cohort discovery, study dashboards, research-grade exports, or sponsored prospective studies.
  • Develop recurring institutional revenue only after data quality, scale, and research utility are validated.

Non-negotiable trust conditions

  • Separate, informed, voluntary opt-in consent; declining never removes the core product.
  • De-identification, aggregation thresholds, re-identification risk review, access controls, and audited data-use agreements.
  • Plain-language disclosure of who is using the data, for what purpose, and what participants receive in return.
  • No sale of identifiable health records and no research program before privacy, legal, security, and independent governance review.
